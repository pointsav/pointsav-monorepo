# Tenant Taxonomy and Development Classes

## Lede

Every Composition on this site belongs to one of five tenant categories, and every Woodfine Building belongs to one of several named Development Classes. Neither taxonomy is ad hoc — both are canonical vocabulary used consistently across Woodfine's own internal glossary, not invented for this site.

---

## The five tenant categories

This Library's Compositions are organised into five core tenant categories: **Academic, Business, Civic, Laboratory, and Medical.** Woodfine's own internal glossary confirms this is the standing taxonomy for office tenants generally, and separately names a subset of these — Academic, Medical, and Civic — as "National Tenants": revenue-driving occupants that drive Mix-of-Use leasing strategy at the portfolio level. Each category corresponds to a distinct Professional Office subtype (Professional Office — Academic, Business, Laboratory, Academic, Medical), each with its own space-planning requirements: a laboratory Composition's programme is not interchangeable with a business-office Composition's, even where both are nominally "office" space.

This taxonomy is also why several data items tracked elsewhere in this project remain open rather than filled with placeholder numbers: real zone-depth data for the Civic category, and the Z2/Z3 depths for Professional Office generally, are not yet confirmed from a real source, and this Library does not fabricate figures to fill that gap.

## Development Classes

At the building level, Woodfine organises its portfolio into named Development Classes (also called Building Typologies): **Professional Centres, Suburban Office, Tech Industrial, Retail Select, Vertical Warehouses,** and **Parking Structures.** Each Development Class carries its own siting rules — for example, certain typologies are sited only within a defined distance of a Power Centre — reflecting that the classes are a real portfolio-planning system, not a marketing label.

A Development Class functions as a **Prototype**, with **Sub-Prototypes** as its specific variants. The **Rollout Program** is the mechanism by which a Prototype, once proven on one Woodfine Building, is repeated across the portfolio — the same underlying rationale that makes a consistent, reusable Object and Composition catalog valuable across an entire portfolio of buildings rather than useful only on a single project.

## How this connects to Key Plans and Tiles

The Development Class a building belongs to determines which Tile types apply to it. The companion TOPIC, "Key Plans and Tiles," notes that for at least two Development Classes specifically — Tech Industrial and Retail Select — the Tile and Floor Plate levels collapse into one, with no intermediate step. This is a real, named exception in Woodfine's own methodology, tied directly to Development Class, not a general rule that applies everywhere.
